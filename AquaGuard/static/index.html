⚗️ What is pH?

          
pH shows how acidic or alkaline water is on a 0–14 scale (7 is neutral). Drinking water is generally considered
          acceptable around 6.5–8.5. Very low pH (acidic) water can corrode pipes and release metals; very high pH can taste bitter.
          Mining runoff often lowers pH.

        
🌫️ What is Turbidity?

          
Turbidity measures how cloudy water is, caused by suspended particles like silt, sand and mud (measured in NTU).
          Cloudy water can hide and protect germs, so higher turbidity usually means the water needs filtration before drinking.

        
🧂 What is TDS?

          
Total Dissolved Solids (ppm) is the total amount of dissolved minerals, salts and other substances. A moderate level is normal,
          but very high TDS can taste salty/bitter and may indicate excessive minerals or contamination — for example dissolved solids
          washed in from mining areas.

        
🌡️ Why does temperature matter?

          
Water temperature affects how dissolved substances behave and how fast germs multiply. A sudden temperature change can signal
          that something new entered the source (e.g., warm industrial discharge) — it is a useful early-warning indicator.

        
🧫 Why is microbial testing important?

          
The four parameters on this dashboard cannot detect bacteria, viruses or parasites. Water can look clear and still be
          microbiologically unsafe. Regular laboratory testing (e.g., faecal coliform/E. coli tests) is essential before water is
          declared safe to drink.

        
⛏ Why mining-affected water needs site-specific testing?

          
Mining areas can release metals (iron, manganese, arsenic and others), acids and fine particles into water. These contaminants
          vary from site to site and cannot be identified by pH/turbidity/TDS alone — specialized laboratory analysis is required,
          and treatment must be chosen to target the actual contaminants found.

        
🛠️ Why choose purification technology based on analysis?

          
Each treatment stage targets different problems: sediment filters remove particles, activated carbon reduces odour and some
          organic chemicals, disinfection targets microbes, and specialized media or membranes address specific dissolved contaminants.
          Choosing technology without knowing the actual contamination wastes money and may leave the real hazard untreated.

        
📊 About the Water Quality Score

          
The score on this dashboard is a prototype demonstration metric computed from the four simulated parameters.
          It is intuitive for community communication but is not an officially certified drinking-water standard and should not
          replace laboratory results.
